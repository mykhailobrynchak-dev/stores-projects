Headline (Baseline → B). Both networks improved unit economics: CPO fell (ANRI €2.59→€2.34, −10%; TAISTRA €2.24→€1.86, −17%), average client–store distance dropped, and the official CP Margin % improved — ANRI −6.4% → −2.3% (+4.1 pp), TAISTRA −4.6% → +0.6% (+5.2 pp, crossing into positive). CP L2 (excl. demand incentives) followed the same path: ANRI −16.2%→−8.5%, TAISTRA −6.9%→−1.8%. ANRI also grew strongly in volume (+65% orders/day), while TAISTRA held volume flat at higher efficiency.
Caveats. Baseline is a long ramp-up window (May–Jul) and B is August, so seasonality and organic growth are mixed in with the radius effect (esp. for the growing ANRI network). CP Margin is still slightly negative for ANRI (delivery partly subsidised — courier cost + incentives exceed commission + fees), but TAISTRA reached breakeven in August. The margin trend was already improving week-over-week before the radius change, so part of the gain is the ongoing ramp, not radius alone.

Takeaways

  • The radius programme did not hurt unit economics — from Baseline to current (B), CPO and delivery distance fell and the official CP Margin improved for both networks, while ANRI scaled volume. TAISTRA crossed into a positive CP Margin (+0.6%) in August.
  • CPO ↔ CP Margin link is moderately negative at store level (r=): lower courier cost is a real driver of margin, but incentives and commission mix still matter. Managing incentive spend matters alongside radius/CPO.
  • Watch-outs: TAISTRA Ternopil is the one city where CP Margin fell (0.2%→−3.3%) and CPO did not improve — the wider radius there added cost without margin, so review its radii locally. TAISTRA late-rate also ticked up. ANRI is still slightly margin-negative overall — trimming incentives / lifting commission is the path to breakeven.
  • For a clean read free of seasonality/ramp, compare like-for-like calendar windows (e.g., Aug-2026 vs Aug-2025) once more history accrues.
