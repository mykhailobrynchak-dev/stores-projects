Headline (Baseline → After). Both networks improved unit economics after the radius customization: CPO fell (ANRI €2.59→€2.34, −10%; TAISTRA €2.24→€1.88, −16%), the average client–store distance dropped sharply (ANRI 3.63→2.56 km, TAISTRA 2.42→1.68 km), and the official CP Margin % improved — ANRI -6.4% → -2.3% (+4.1 pp), TAISTRA -4.6% → +0.4% (+5.0 pp) (crossing into positive). Deliveries shifted markedly toward shorter distances (see chart) — the long 4–6 km+ tail shrank.
Caveats. Baseline is a long ramp-up window (May–Jul) and After is late-Jul–Aug, so seasonality and organic growth are mixed in with the radius effect (esp. for the growing ANRI network, whose margin was already improving week-over-week). CP Margin is still slightly negative for ANRI (delivery partly subsidised); TAISTRA reached breakeven.

Takeaways

  • The radius programme improved unit economics — CPO and delivery distance fell and the official CP Margin improved for both networks. TAISTRA crossed into a positive CP Margin after the change.
  • Distances compressed: the share of long 4 km+ deliveries dropped for both networks, which is the direct mechanism behind lower CPO.
  • Watch-out — TAISTRA Ternopil: the only city where CP Margin fell (small sample, widened radius added cost without margin). Review its radii locally. ANRI is still slightly margin-negative overall — trimming incentives / lifting commission is the path to breakeven.
